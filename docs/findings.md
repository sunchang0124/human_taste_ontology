# HTO — findings

## 1. Prior-art search

Verified 2026-09-17 against the OBO Foundry registry (267 ontologies) and the
EBI Ontology Lookup Service (OLS). No ontology of human taste perception
exists. What does exist, and what it does *not* cover:

**Walkthrough page:** https://claude.ai/artifact/L5wdjXo15GqfinKaixEtPs — the gap, the three layers, a worked tasting, and what the build does and does not demonstrate.

- **FoodOn** — covers foods (including citrus varieties such as satsuma,
  yuzu and pomelo), not what a person perceives when tasting them.
- **FOBI** — food biomarkers, not perception.
- **CDNO** — nutrition composition, not perception.
- **FIDEO** — food–drug interactions, not perception.
- **PATO** — has `PATO:0002474 bitter` and nothing else: no sweet, sour,
  salty or umami quality term.
- **ChEBI** — has `CHEBI:74783 astringent`, but as a *chemical role*
  (something a substance has), not a perceptual quality (something a taster
  experiences). Using it for the latter would be a category error.
- **SNOMED CT** — has `110365009` for aftertaste, but nowhere else in the
  OBO/OLS ecosystem does aftertaste appear.
- Kokumi, oleogustus and mouthfeel appear **nowhere** in OLS.
- No resource anywhere models a tasting event — who tasted what, and what
  they perceived — as a queryable, instance-level thing.

This is the gap HTO fills. Everything else — foods, chemistry, receptor
genetics, anatomy, organism traits, disorders of taste — HTO imports rather
than redefines; see `src/external_terms.tsv` (56 terms) and
`README.md`'s "What HTO reuses" section. Most of those 56 are staged for the
Matsuyama tasting and for the bridge axioms rather than load-bearing today:
12 are referenced by an HTO axiom in `src/templates/*.tsv`, 3 FoodOn terms are
referenced by the instance data through `data/raw/example_samples.csv`, 25
appear as objects in the SSSOM bridge, and 24 are declared and imported but
not yet referenced by anything.

## 2. The PATO gap, measured

`docs/pato-gap-report.md`, generated by `scripts/gap_report.py` from
`mappings/hto-pato.sssom.tsv`, checks every one of HTO's 34 quality-lexicon
terms for an exact PATO match:

**33 of 34 HTO quality terms have no PATO equivalent.** The single exception
is `HTO:0000114 bitterness`, mapped `skos:exactMatch` to `PATO:0002474
bitter`. The four basic tastes PATO lacks — sweetness, sourness, saltiness,
umami — are the subject of the new-term request in
`docs/term-requests/pato-basic-tastes.md`.

## 3. The published re-annotation is incomplete, and why

`data/raw/published_tas2r38_prop.csv` re-annotates a real, verified study:
PMID:37242298 (Aoki et al. 2023, *Nutrients* 15(10):2415,
doi:10.3390/nu15102415, PMC10222862), a PROP detection-threshold study in
adults in Japan by TAS2R38 diplotype. The CSV row for each diplotype carries:

- the real per-group sample sizes from the paper's Table 2 (PAV/PAV n=24,
  PAV/AVI n=43, AVI/AVI n=12, total n=79 completing the modified 2AFC/QUEST
  procedure), and
- the real pairwise significance from the paper's Results section (one-way
  ANOVA F(2,76)=163.32, MSE=12.940, p<0.001 overall; PAV/PAV vs AVI/AVI
  p<0.001; PAV/AVI vs AVI/AVI p<0.001; PAV/PAV vs PAV/AVI p<0.01).

It carries **no numeric threshold values**. The paper's only per-genotype
threshold figures are shown graphically, on a log scale, in Figure 3 (a
scatter plot) and Figure 4 (a boxplot); neither the compute node building
this ontology nor its controller could retrieve those images (network access
during the build was restricted to `eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`, and
`www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`, `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` and `mdpi.com` all refused or
redirected the fetch). Rather than estimate a number by eye from an
unreadable log-scale figure, the `threshold_mmol_per_l` column is left
empty for all three rows. Consequently:

```
$ python3 scripts/published2rdf.py data/raw/published_tas2r38_prop.csv data/rdf/published.ttl
wrote data/rdf/published.ttl: 0 threshold data
```

**Zero `HTO:0000009 detection threshold datum` individuals are emitted from
the published data.** This is a limitation, not a success, and it is stated
here plainly rather than papered over. The CSV row is otherwise complete and
ready: anyone with access to the article's PDF (to read the figures directly,
or to obtain the underlying numbers from the authors) can fill in
`threshold_mmol_per_l` and rerun `scripts/published2rdf.py` with no other
change required.

## 4. Process note: citation verification

The implementation plan (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-17-hto.md`) was
drafted with PMIDs written from memory. Of the eleven PMIDs its author wrote
that way, **ten were fabricated** — real-looking identifiers that, when
checked, actually resolve to unrelated papers (soil chemistry, plant
physiology, lymphoma and fertility, and similar subjects with no connection
to taste perception). The clearest example is the plan's citation for the
published TAS2R38/PROP re-annotation, `PMID:37432335`, together with
fabricated numeric thresholds attached to it — neither the identifier nor
the numbers survived verification.

Every citation now present in the built ontology (`src/templates/*.tsv`) and
in the instance data was independently verified against NCBI eutils before
being used, and several differ from the plan's originals as a direct result
(for example, `HTO:0000125 starchy taste` cites `PMID:27553043`, not the
plan's `PMID:27590743`; the published re-annotation cites the real
`PMID:37242298`, not the plan's fabricated `PMID:37432335`). This is why the
ontology can be trusted, and it is a warning to anyone who goes back to read
the plan document for background: it is kept as historical text and still
contains the unverified, largely fabricated identifiers. Do not cite
anything from the plan document directly; cite the verified templates and
mappings instead.

## 5. What the example tasting data demonstrates

`data/raw/example_tasting.csv` is **invented, synthetic example data**,
written to exercise the pipeline end to end (converter, competency
questions, QC), not a real tasting. It must never be cited as evidence about
anyone's actual taste perception. It contains six participant codes (P01–P05
consenting, P99 declining consent) and three citrus samples per consenting
participant (A, B, C), which is enough to exercise:

- the `consent` gate in `scripts/sheet2rdf.py` (P99's row produces no
  percept assertions because `consent` is `no`);
- the temporal-quality model's *mechanics*: each row carries an
  `aftertaste_present` flag and a `minutes_since_opening` value, and sample C
  is consistently recorded at 90 minutes while samples A and B are
  consistently recorded at 5 minutes, so `cq06.rq` can retrieve every
  temporal-quality assertion (`HTO:0000152 aftertaste`, under
  `HTO:0000150 temporal taste quality`) with its sample and its elapsed time,
  exercising the `minutes since preparation` property end to end. Bitterness
  ratings are also higher on the 90-minute sample than on the 5-minute ones,
  which is the shape `HTO:0000154 delayed bitterness` describes.
  **This is not a within-sample time series**: no
  single sample is ever rated at two different elapsed times by the same
  taster, so the data cannot and does not show any one sample's bitterness
  actually rising over time — only that a later-tasted, different sample
  happens to be rated more bitter. A genuine test of the delayed-bitterness
  claim needs a repeated-measures sheet, tasting the *same* poured sample
  again after a delay;
- confounders (a current smoker, a participant with recent smell loss);
  and
- all eight competency questions and the two QC queries.

`scripts/sheet2rdf.py data/raw/example_tasting.csv` produces 70 taste
percept assertions from this file: 60 rated qualities (four per consenting
row) plus 10 aftertaste-presence assertions, one for each consenting row whose
`aftertaste_present` column reads `yes`. Aftertaste is collected as a
presence, not a magnitude, so those 10 carry no intensity rating.
`data/raw/example_samples.csv` ties each sample letter to the FoodOn class it
was prepared from, which is what makes `cq01.rq` a query about food rather
than about opaque sample URIs. **The real Matsuyama sheet has not been
collected yet.** When it is, it replaces this file as the input to the same
converter; nothing about the pipeline needs to change.

## 6. Competency question results

From `python3 scripts/validate.py` against `hto.owl`, the SSSOM bridge
(`mappings/mappings.ttl`, generated by `scripts/mappings2rdf.py`) and the
instance data (`data/rdf/example.ttl`, `data/rdf/published.ttl`).

The "scope" column matters. A **TBox-only** query reads the ontology and the
mapping bridge and nothing else: it cannot fail because instance data is
missing, because it never touches instance data. Deleting both instance graphs
leaves every TBox-only row passing with exactly the same count. Only the
**ABox** rows are evidence that the pipeline ingested anything.

| Query | The question it actually answers | Scope | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cq01.rq` | For each FoodOn food class a sample was prepared from, which qualities were reported and by how many distinct tasters? | ABox | PASS — 15 rows |
| `cq02.rq` | What is the mean bitterness intensity per PROP taster status? | ABox | PASS — 3 rows |
| `cq03.rq` | For which samples is the range between the lowest and the highest bitterness rating greater than 20 scale points? (Not a standard deviation: a min/max spread.) | ABox | PASS — 3 rows |
| `cq04.rq` | Which tastants are associated with the bitterness subtypes HTO distinguishes, via the ChEBI mappings? (It does **not** say which tastants are in the sampled juices, and it does **not** consult TAS2R38 agonist data, which HTO does not carry.) | TBox only | PASS — 5 rows |
| `cq05.rq` | Which HTO qualities have no `skos:exactMatch` to a PATO term? | TBox only | PASS — 33 rows |
| `cq06.rq` | Retrieve every assertion of a temporal taste quality (under `HTO:0000150`), with the sample and the minutes elapsed since preparation. | ABox | PASS — 10 rows |
| `cq07.rq` | Which participants are typed with a taste confounder that would exclude them from a bitterness analysis? | ABox | PASS — 2 rows |
| `cq08.rq` | For each basic taste, which GO perception process (from the `HTO:0000061` restriction) and which OBA sensitivity trait (from the mappings) does it link to? Both are `OPTIONAL`, so a missing link shows as an unbound column rather than dropping the taste from the answer. | TBox only | PASS — 5 rows |
| `qc_label_def.rq` | QC: every HTO term carries an `rdfs:label`. **Labels only** — despite the file name, it does not check definitions; those are enforced by `robot report`'s `missing_definition` rule. | TBox only | PASS — 0 rows (expected none) |
| `qc_orphan.rq` | QC: every HTO class has at least one *asserted* parent, with three allow-listed roots (`HTO:0000002` taster role, `HTO:0000003` taste stimulus, `HTO:0000018` palate cleansing process). | TBox only | PASS — 0 rows (expected none) |

Two caveats on the QC queries, stated plainly because the file names promise
more than the queries deliver:

- `qc_orphan.rq` checks *has an asserted parent*, which is strictly weaker than
  *is reachable from the root*. A cycle, or a cluster of HTO classes parented
  only within itself and joined to nothing above, would satisfy it. The
  stronger property is not checked because three HTO classes are deliberate
  roots with no suitable imported parent, so there is no single root to be
  reachable from; the allow-list records that decision rather than hiding it.
- `make report`'s zero-violations result is **scoped to HTO terms** by
  `--base-iri "http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/HTO_"`. Imported terms from GO,
  ChEBI, FoodOn, PATO, OBA, UBERON, HP, IAO, OBI and CL are excluded from the
  report, so it says nothing about their quality; it says that HTO's own terms
  are clean.

All eight competency questions and both QC checks pass: `0 failing`.
`make report` (ROBOT report against `src/report_profile.txt`) returns zero
violations at ERROR, WARN or INFO level for HTO terms.
`python3 -m pytest tests/ -q` passes 30 tests with 1 deselected: the
deselected one is `tests/test_imports.py::test_script_is_idempotent`, marked
`network` because it rebuilds the import module from the live OLS4 API, so the
default suite runs offline. `python3 -m pytest -m network` runs it.

## 7. Limitations

Expanded from the design spec, §11:

- **Twenty tasters is a demonstration, not a study.** The Matsuyama tasting
  design calls for roughly twenty participants across four to six juices.
  That is enough to show the schema and pipeline work end to end; it is not
  a statistically powered study and supports no claim about the Japanese
  population, or any population, at large. Nothing in this repository
  should be read as such a claim.
- **PROP filter-paper strips approximate TAS2R38 status; they are not
  genotyping.** Taster status (`HTO:0000011` and its subclasses) is recorded
  as a *phenotype* from the strip assay. HTO *provides a property* for
  relating a taster status to a diplotype — `HTO:0000064 typically associated
  with diplotype`, deliberately an association rather than an equivalence,
  because strip response and diplotype correlate but do not determine one
  another one-to-one. **No such link is asserted yet**: `HTO:0000064` has zero
  uses in this release, in the ontology and in both instance graphs. The
  property is the modelling commitment; the content behind it has not been
  curated. No triple in HTO asserts logical equivalence between a phenotype
  and a genotype.
- **The published re-annotation carries no threshold numbers** (§3 above):
  it demonstrates that the schema accommodates literature data, not that it
  contains literature data yet.
- **The example tasting data is synthetic** (§5 above) and must not be cited
  as real perceptual evidence.
- **Chemesthesis is included but flagged.** Astringency, pungency, cooling,
  tingling and carbonation bite are trigeminal sensations, not gustatory
  ones. They are included under a distinct parent
  (`HTO:0000140 chemesthetic quality`) because sensory panels routinely
  record them and because astringency is central to how citrus juice is
  actually described. They are **not** subclasses of `HTO:0000110 basic taste
  quality`, and each is flagged as trigeminal rather than gustatory. They are,
  however, subclasses of `HTO:0000100 perceptual taste quality`, the root of
  the whole quality lexicon, whose definition covers anything realised when a
  human perceiver tastes an entity: HTO places them in the same broad
  perceptual space it uses for taste, and withholds only the basic-taste
  claim.
- **Contested tastes are marked as contested.** Kokumi, oleogustus
  (fattiness), metallic taste, calcium taste and starchy taste sit under a
  separate parent (`HTO:0000120 candidate taste quality`) with the
  literature controversy recorded in each definition. HTO does not take a
  scientific position it cannot defend.
- **Descriptor terms are culture-bound.** Labels are in English, with
  Japanese synonyms recorded where a participant used one; the lexicon does
  not claim, and was never intended to claim, cross-cultural completeness.
  A different tasting culture would likely need additional or different
  quality terms.
- **The PURL will not resolve.** `http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/HTO_*`
  IRIs are minted and used throughout, but OBO Foundry registration has not
  happened; the PURL redirect will not work until it does.
